The EU's Code of Practice says it plainly: no one marking method meets every requirement. So each file gets layers that back each other up.

The trap most AI apps fall into

Your model provider watermarks. Your pipeline deletes it.

  1. The model signs the image. The big labs now attach C2PA credentials to their output.
  2. Your app resizes it, converts it to WebP, or composites a logo. The metadata is gone.
  3. Open-weight models never marked anything. Self-hosted image, voice and video stacks start with nothing.
  4. The obligation is still yours. "Our vendor handles it" doesn't answer an enterprise security review.

Straight answers

Questions teams ask first

My model provider already watermarks. Why do I need this?

Their mark often doesn't survive your own processing, and open-weight models don't add one. You also carry your own obligations as the provider of your app. Sigil marks the file you actually ship and keeps the proof.

What doesn't the watermark survive?

Cropping, rescaling and deliberate removal attacks. It survives re-encoding (JPEG down to quality 50, WebP) and brightness changes. That's why it's always paired with a signed manifest and a registry record. Rescale-robust marking is on the roadmap.

What about text?

Statistical text watermarks have to be applied while the model generates. Sigil logs a fingerprint of your text that still matches after light edits, adds a localized disclosure when you want one, and tells you to switch on your model provider's text watermark as the robust layer.

Is this legal advice?

No. It's engineering that follows the published rules and codes of practice, with every rule sourced and versioned. Run your final position past counsel.

Is it open source?

Yes. The CLI, SDK and rules engine are Apache-2.0. The hosted API, branded detector and evidence store are the paid product.
